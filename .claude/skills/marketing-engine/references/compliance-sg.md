# Compliance: Singapore health advertising + platform policy hot spots

Source of truth for SG rules is the SG pack in `.claude/skills/higgsfield-creative-director/references/localisation.md` and the brand files (`brands/physio-spot.md`, `brands/heal360.md`). This file turns them into a checklist; if anything here seems to conflict, the SG pack wins. Not legal advice - confirm which codes apply to each entity with counsel.

## 1. Which rules apply (per SG pack)
- HCS (Advertisement) Regulations 2021 (Reg 14) for licensable healthcare services; SMC Ethical Code and Ethical Guidelines (ECEG) for doctors; the relevant professional code for allied health (physiotherapists are registered with the Allied Health Professions Council - check its code of conduct on advertising). Verify per clinic whether it is licensable under HCSA or covered by the professional council's code.
- General: ASAS Singapore Code of Advertising Practice (legal, decent, honest, truthful).
- Applies to: Physio Spot, heal360, and any health claim on Klaus's personal channels that promotes a clinic. Power Protein (Vietnam): load the VN pack and confirm local food/health-claim rules.

## 2. Pre-publish checklist (health brands; every caption, video line, on-screen text, thumbnail, ad)
```
[ ] No testimonials or endorsements: no patient quotes, reviews, star ratings, before/after, patient photos/video, "X patients helped" framed as proof
[ ] No paid or incentivised reviews; never ask for reviews in exchange for anything
[ ] No superlatives/comparatives: best, leading, No.1, most trusted, top, #1-rated, better than <clinic/doctor>
[ ] No inducements: free consult, discount, promo, giveaway, "limited slots", price-led urgency
[ ] No guarantees: cure, permanent fix, 100%, "never again", "in 3 sessions"
[ ] Every medical/statistical claim substantiable: source in SOURCES.md (peer-reviewed / guideline / official stats); phrase as "can help", "evidence suggests", "for some men"
[ ] Dramatised skits are clearly fiction/dramatisation; never framed as a real patient's story; actors not presented as patients
[ ] Case examples are generic composites ("a common pattern we see"), de-identified, not outcome-boasting
[ ] Practitioner credentials stated accurately (registered physiotherapist), no invented titles ("specialist" only if formally recognised)
[ ] Educational disclaimer where useful: "Educational content, not individual medical advice."
[ ] CTA is neutral: "Book an assessment", "WhatsApp us to ask about appointments"
[ ] Lead capture / newsletter: PDPA consent for marketing messages, purpose stated, unsubscribe; check DNC Registry before SMS/calls; Spam Control Act basics for email/SMS (unsubscribe facility, sender identity)
[ ] Client stories on personal channels: written consent, de-identified (creator-brain rules), and still never used as clinic testimonials
```

## 3. Platform policy hot spots: sexual-health content
Platforms restrict sexual content and some health targeting. These rules are separate from SG law and change often; check the current Meta Advertising Standards, TikTok Advertising Policies / Community Guidelines, Google Ads Healthcare & Sexual content policies, YouTube Community Guidelines before paid runs.

| Hot spot | Risk | Safe alternative |
|---|---|---|
| Ads that assert or imply the viewer's condition ("Do you have ED?", "Your erection problems…") | Meta "personal attributes" policy disallows implying knowledge of a person's health condition | Third person / general: "ED is common and treatable", "Many men notice changes in erectile function" |
| Performance / pleasure framing ("last longer in bed", "boost your sex drive") | Ad rejection; adult-content limits; also unproven-claim risk | Function and health: "pelvic floor physiotherapy for erectile function and ejaculatory control" |
| Explicit or suggestive visuals (bedroom, underwear, body-part close-ups, fruit/eggplant innuendo in ads) | Ad rejection, age-restriction, reduced distribution | Clinic setting, anatomy diagrams, deadpan reaction faces, implication via dialogue (ledger: implied, never crude) |
| Slang/crude words in spoken audio, captions or on-screen text for ads | Rejection, limited reach | Clinical terms in ads; crude hooks only in organic posts and only in post-production (CLAUDE.md hard rule) |
| Algospeak / misspelling to dodge moderation (s3x, seggs, "unalive") | Bypassing moderation is a studio hard line (ledger mature-edge) | Use plain clinical wording; accept an 18+ setting or lower reach rather than evade |
| Before/after or "results" imagery | Health-policy rejection + SG testimonial rule | Process demos, mechanism animations |
| Targeting by sexual health interest / minors | Ads must target 18+ for sexual health on most platforms | Age 25+ RoT for Physio Spot ads; broad men's health interests |
| Medical misinformation risk (claims against established guidance) | YouTube/TikTok medical misinformation policies | Align with guidelines; cite sources in description |
| Health landing pages | Ad review checks the destination | Same compliance on the landing page; no free-consult banners |

Organic tips: set Physio Spot content to the platform's mature/18+ option only when the piece is genuinely adult; keep thumbnails and first frames non-suggestive; put the education (end card) in every skit.
