# Marketing strategy and effectiveness

The strategist, `/strategy`, `/calendar` and `/ad` use this to plan with evidence rather than folklore. It was researched on 2026-10-25; sources are listed in `research/sources-agency-playbooks.md` §4.

Labels:
- **[contested]**: open debate.
- **[verify]**: confirm against the primary source before quoting it to a client.

Most of the big-brand evidence comes from large FMCG/B2C brands in Western markets. TITAN's clients are mostly small, local and regulated, so scale every heuristic to the client's size, category and market.

**Evidence ladder:**
1. The client's own incrementality or lift results.
2. Large databases: IPA Databank, Nielsen meta-analyses, Ehrenberg-Bass laws.
3. Platform marketing science.
4. Practitioner frameworks.
5. Folklore.

## 1. Diagnosis → strategy → tactics (Ritson)
**Rule:** no skill proposes a channel or content plan until the diagnosis in the §6 one-page strategy is filled in. If data is missing, state the assumption. Also apply Ritson's "bothism": brand *and* performance, long *and* short.

**Diagnosis toolkit:**
- **Voice of customer (VoC):** 8–12 customer interviews of about 20 minutes each. Also mine Google reviews (yours and competitors'), Reddit, HardwareZone, Lowyat, TikTok and IG comments, WhatsApp enquiry logs and sales notes. Keep the wording verbatim.
- **Competitor audit:** Meta Ad Library, TikTok Creative Center, Google Ads Transparency Center, competitors' Google Business Profiles (GBP), and their Shopee/Lazada listings and reviews.
- **Category entry points (CEPs; Romaniuk).** These are the buying situations in which the brand must come to mind. Find them with the W's: Why, When, Where, With/for whom, With what, How feeling.
  - Mental availability is measured with three numbers. **Mental Market Share** is the brand's share of all brand–CEP links. **Mental Penetration** is the percentage of category buyers who link the brand to at least one CEP. **Network Size** is how many CEPs the brand is linked to among those buyers.
  - A vendor meta-analysis reports Mental Market Share correlating with sales share at r≈0.83 (vendor data, not peer-reviewed).
  - **Rule:** every brand file lists 8–15 ranked CEPs, and each creative brief targets 1–2 of them.
- **Brand-health baseline:** awareness, consideration, Mental Market Share / Mental Penetration, branded search, reviews (see §5.4).

**Segmentation, targeting, positioning (STP):**
- Segment on needs and situations, not demographics alone.
- **Target strategically, reach broadly within the category.** Brands grow mainly through **penetration**, meaning more category buyers (Sharp). Loyalty follows **Double Jeopardy**: small brands have fewer buyers, and those buyers are slightly less loyal.
- **[contested] "Loyalty is a myth."** In repeat-service and subscription businesses (physio courses of care, protein subscriptions, SaaS), retention economics are real. Fix churn operationally, but don't let retention marketing crowd out acquisition.

**Positioning (Dunford, *Obviously Awesome*), written in this order, never starting from a tagline:**
1. Competitive alternatives, including doing nothing and DIY.
2. Unique attributes.
3. The value those attributes enable.
4. Best-fit customers (who cares a lot).
5. Market category, as the frame of reference.
6. Relevant trends, only if they are real.

**Objectives ladder:**
- Business: revenue, profit, patients per month, lifetime value, payback.
- Marketing: penetration, new customers, share, price premium, conversion rate.
- Comms: reach in the target, mental availability, branded search, distinctive-asset attribution.
- Campaigns that set hard business objectives were more effective than those optimising intermediate metrics (Binet & Field 2013).
- **Every brief carries one business KPI, 1–2 leading indicators and one guardrail** (for example a CPA ceiling, or compliance incidents = 0).

## 2. Brand building vs activation
**The evidence:**
- **Binet & Field (IPA 2013, 2017):**
  - Brand building means emotional, broad-reach, long-term work. It produces larger, slower, more durable growth and more pricing power.
  - Activation means rational, targeted, short-term work. It produces spikes that decay.
  - The optimum for established B2C brands is about **60/40 brand/activation**.
- **Effectiveness in Context (2018):** the ratio varies by category, life stage, size and business model. Pull the category table; don't quote 60/40 everywhere.
- **B2B:** about 46/54 brand/activation (LinkedIn B2B Institute 2019) **[verify]**. Use "roughly half and half".
- **95:5 rule (Dawes, Ehrenberg-Bass):** only about 5% of B2B buyers are in-market in a given quarter. Activation-only work misses the 95% who will buy later.
- **[contested] 60/40 for small or local brands.** The databank skews toward large brands, and Sharp calls the 60/40 figure unscientific. Binet himself says small or new brands with low awareness, short cash runways, or online-led categories often need more activation, and the brand share should rise as the business scales.
- **[contested] "Performance is just harvesting."** Activation partly captures demand that already exists. eBay's branded search ads had near-zero incremental effect (Blake, Nosko & Tadelis 2015, *Econometrica*).

**TITAN starting splits.** These are TITAN heuristics, not published figures; revisit them quarterly against branded-search trend, new-customer share and blended CAC.

| Situation | Brand : activation |
|---|---|
| Local clinic, under S$5k/month, capacity-constrained | 20–30 : 70–80 (demand capture pays the bills; brand building through always-on organic video and distinctive assets) |
| Clinic scaling to multiple outlets | 35–45 : 55–65 |
| Early direct-to-consumer (D2C): protein, fitness apparel | 30–40 : 60–70 |
| Established D2C, over S$50k/month | 50–60 : 40–50 |
| B2B AI agency for SMEs | ~45–50 : 50–55 |

**Reach and targeting:**
- **Reach beats frequency** within the category. Light buyers and non-buyers are where growth comes from.
- **Excess share of voice (ESOV):** about 0.5 share points of growth per year for every 10 points of share of voice above market share (Field 2009). Local SMEs can use share of local search impressions and social reach as proxies.
- **Precision targeting is overrated.** Across about 450 sales-effect studies (Nielsen/NCSolutions 2023), sales lift came from: creative ~49%, brand 21%, reach 14%, targeting 11%, recency 5%.
- A single-outlet clinic has a real geographic limit (people travel about 20–30 minutes for routine care). Narrow by geography, but don't micro-target demographics inside that radius.

## 3. Distinctive assets, fame, emotion, creative quality
- **Distinctive assets (Romaniuk 2018).** These are logo, colour, character, shape, sound, tagline, font, spokesperson and pack. Score each on **fame** (share of category buyers who link it to the brand) and **uniqueness** (share who link it to the brand and no competitor).
  - High fame + high uniqueness: the asset can stand in for the brand name.
  - Low fame + high uniqueness: invest in it.
  - High fame + low uniqueness: avoid it, because it's generic.
  - **Rules:**
    - Pick 1–3 assets and keep them for years; most brands quit before buyers learn them.
    - Show the brand within the first 2–3 seconds of short video, and repeat it.
    - Prefer non-verbal assets (colour, character, sonic logo), which survive sound-off viewing and SG/MY/VN language switches.
    - Real people (the founder, the head physio) make strong assets, within SG health advertising rules.
- **Fame and emotion.** Emotional, fame-driving campaigns beat rational ones on long-term hard business effects (Binet & Field 2013). Field's *Crisis in Creative Effectiveness* (2019) found effectiveness declining as short-termism grew.
- **Creative quality is the biggest controllable lever:** about 47–49% of sales contribution (Nielsen/NCSolutions). Spend disproportionate effort on concepts, pre-testing and iteration. Never cut creative below the level a test-and-learn cycle needs.
- **Right-brain creative (Orlando Wood, *Lemon*/*Look Out*).** The neuroscience framing is **[contested]**, but the System1 correlations are better grounded.

  | Use more | Use less |
  |---|---|
  | Characters with agency, recurring brand characters | Disembodied product shots |
  | Dialogue | Flat monologue or VO only |
  | A sense of place, scenes that unfold | Isolated, decontextualised frames |
  | Humour, wit, melody, implicit meaning | Text walls, "3 reasons why" overlays, choppy beat-only edits |

  This fits the studio's skit and story pipeline. Keep explicit benefit text for the end card.
- **Behavioural levers in the messy middle (Google 2020):** category heuristics, power of now, social proof, scarcity, authority, power of free. **SG health:** testimonials, "free", superlative authority and scarcity are restricted. Use category heuristics ("what to look for in a physio") and power of now ("same-week slots", stated plainly) instead.

## 4. Channel planning
| Channel | Job | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Owned** (site, GBP, email/WhatsApp lists, marketplace stores) | Conversion, retention, data | Cheapest incremental revenue; GBP is the local storefront |
| **Organic social** | Distinctive-asset repetitions, trust, creative R&D | Low, volatile reach. Use it as the test bed and put paid behind the winners |
| **Paid social** | Reach + activation | Creative is the main targeting signal (Advantage+, Smart+) |
| **Search / marketplace search** | Demand capture | Branded search is often weakly incremental; generic and local intent are where the value is |
| **YouTube / CTV / programmatic** | Cheap reach | Brand bursts at larger budgets |
| **Creators** | Reach + trust + creative supply | Wies, Bleier & Edeling (*JM* 2023, 802 campaigns): **inverted U, so mid-tier wins**; customisation helps both small and large creators |
| **PR / earned** | Fame, authority | Expert commentary in the Straits Times, CNA, Mothership, podcasts |
| **Community / partnerships** | Penetration in niches | Run clubs, gyms, corporate wellness, SME associations (SBF, ASME) |
| **Offline** (clinics) | Proximity | Condo/HDB flyers (check MCST rules), GP and gym referral partnerships, booths |

**Creator rules:**
- Build a portfolio of mid-tier creators with brief freedom.
- Buy whitelisting/Spark rights so winners can be amplified with paid media.
- Judge creators on audience fit and incremental results, not followers.
- **SG health:** creators can appear as educators only, never delivering testimonials or endorsements.

**SEA specifics:**
- **WhatsApp** is the default conversion channel in SG/MY. Use click-to-WhatsApp, catalogues and opt-in broadcasts (PDPA + DNC). Track enquiries as conversions.
- **Marketplaces.** SEA platform e-commerce GMV was about **US$157.6B in 2025 (+23%)** (Momentum Works 2026). Shopee, Lazada and TikTok Shop/Tokopedia hold over 98.8% of it; TikTok Shop's GMV is about 66% of Shopee's.
  - For protein in MY/VN, marketplaces are mandatory. In SG, run D2C + Shopee/Lazada + TikTok Shop.
  - Marketplace ads are activation; TikTok Shop live and affiliate work is both reach and conversion.
- **Xiaohongshu:** reaches Chinese-speaking Singaporeans and expats. It is review-heavy, so testimonial rules bite hardest for clinics; use educational notes (platform-signals.md §5).
- **Vietnam:** Facebook, Zalo, TikTok, Shopee. Advertising Law amendment 75/2025 (in force 1 Jan 2026): influencers must verify claims, disclose sponsorship and take content down within 24 h.
- **UK:** ASA/CAP Code; supplements may only use claims on the GB NHC register; #ad disclosure required.

**SME local engine:**
- **GBP ranking** depends on relevance, distance and prominence (Google). Complete categories and services, post weekly, use real photos, keep name/address/phone consistent, answer Q&A, and put UTMs on the buttons.
- **Reviews.** For HCSA-licensed clinics: let reviews arise organically. Never incentivise them, reproduce them in ads or on the site, or run "review us" campaigns with rewards (MOH Regulation 14). Non-health clients may ask for reviews but never buy fake ones (UK DMCC bans fake reviews).
- **Local SEO and AI answers (AEO):** condition and service pages that answer real VoC questions in plain, structured language; neighbourhood pages only where the clinic is genuinely present.

## 5. Measurement
**Principles:**
- **Correlation is not incrementality.** Observational methods often overstated lift compared with large Facebook randomised trials (Gordon et al. 2019, *Marketing Science*). Ad returns are hard to measure without big experiments (Lewis & Rao 2015, *QJE*).
- Platform-reported conversions double-count. Last-click inflates branded search and retargeting.

**Toolkit by budget:**

| Method | When |
|---|---|
| Platform lift tests (Meta, Google, TikTok) | Mid-size spend per platform |
| Geo / matched-market tests (GeoLift, Meridian geo) | Multi-outlet clinics; SG vs MY |
| MMM: **Google Meridian** (GA 29 Jan 2025) or Meta Robyn | 2+ years of weekly data, ≥5 channels; calibrate with lift results |
| **Holdout / pause tests** | Small budgets. Pause branded search 2–4 weeks in one market; switch off retargeting; compare against control periods |
| "How did you hear about us?" | Always: booking forms and WhatsApp scripts |

**Indicators:**
- **Leading:**
  - Branded search (Google Trends / Search Console): a proxy for mental availability, not itself an incremental channel.
  - Share of local search impressions (GBP Insights).
  - Reach and frequency distribution.
  - Hook and hold rates.
  - Saves, shares and DMs.
  - WhatsApp enquiry volume and quality.
  - Asset recognition.
  - Direct traffic.
- **Lagging:** revenue, new customers, CAC/payback, lifetime value, share.

**SME brand tracking:** a quarterly survey of 200–400 category buyers per market (Milieu in SG, or Pollfish/Toluna) costing about S$1–3k. Ask:
1. Unaided recall.
2. Prompted awareness.
3. A CEP grid: 8–10 CEPs × your brand + 4 competitors (gives Mental Market Share, Mental Penetration, Network Size).
4. Masked-asset recognition ("which brand is this?").
5. Consideration.

Below that budget, track branded search + the "how did you hear" field + social share of voice.

**Testing culture:**
- One hypothesis per test, with a pre-declared metric and minimum detectable effect.
- Test big concept swings before variants.
- Log tests in `marketing/reviews/`, then `/learn`.

## 6. Planning artefacts
### One-page strategy (`marketing/strategy/<brand>.md` header; content-strategy.md §9 continues it)
```
BRAND / MARKET / DATE / OWNER
1 DIAGNOSIS: business objective (numbers) · category size/growth/purchase cycle/% in-market · top 3 VoC insights (verbatim) + ranked CEPs ·
  alternatives incl. do-nothing; white space · brand health baseline · constraints: budget, capacity, compliance pack
2 STRATEGY: primary target (+ who we won't chase) · positioning in Dunford order · 1-3 distinctive assets + rules ·
  objectives ladder (business → marketing → comms + guardrails) · brand:activation split, reach goal, budget
3 TACTICS: offer, price, place (channels/marketplaces), promotion (always-on + bursts) · measurement plan (lift/holdout this quarter) · 90-day roadmap
```
### Campaign brief
```
Business problem + numeric objective · audience + target CEP · audience insight (human truth from VoC) · single-minded proposition ·
reasons to believe (compliant, sourced) · brand role + distinctive assets in first 3 s · tone / right-brain cues ·
mandatories (compliance lines, disclaimers, /sound plan, AI labels) · formats/aspects/channels/flighting ·
KPIs (1 business, 1-2 leading, guardrails) + test design · budget, timing, approvals (spec.json gate)
```

### Always-on and bursts
- **Always-on** (about 60–70% of working media for small brands):
  - GBP and local search
  - Branded and generic search
  - Marketplace ads
  - Low-frequency retargeting
  - 3–5 organic posts per week per core platform
  - One evergreen paid brand creative per platform, refreshed every 4–8 weeks
- **Bursts:** 2–4 a year, 3–6 weeks each, at category moments.
  - **SG:** January resolutions, CNY and pre-CNY gifting, Great Singapore Sale, marathon training leading into the December StanChart Marathon, 9.9/11.11/12.12, Hari Raya, Deepavali, Movember, Q4 corporate budgets.
  - **UK:** January, London Marathon (April), Black Friday.
  - **VN:** Tết, 11.11, 12.12.

### Budget heuristics
- Marketing as a share of revenue **[contested]**: local clinics 3–8%; early D2C 15–30%; established D2C 8–15%; B2B services 5–10%.
- Hold **10–20% for testing**.
- Creative production at least 10–20% of working media; many creatives beat one expensive hero.
- **Capacity gate for clinics:** never buy more demand than there are appointment slots. Throttle activation when utilisation is above about 85%.

**Compliance quick reference:**
- **HCSA advertising rules (Advertisement Regulations 2021):** no testimonials or endorsements, no induced or paid reviews (Regulation 14), only technical awards, only licensees may advertise. The SMC code adds no laudatory claims or inducements.
- **Physiotherapy** is non-licensable under the HCSA, but that is not a loophole:
  - Non-licensees may not claim to "treat" medical conditions.
  - The Medicines (Advertisement and Sale) Act may restrict claims about scheduled conditions **[verify]**.
  - MOH announced a review of the "treat" prohibition for allied health on 7 March 2025 **[verify the outcome before each campaign]**.
  - Describe services and educate; avoid "we treat/cure X".
- **Supplements:** no medicinal claims (SG HSA/ASAS). MY may need MAB approval **[verify]**. VN needs ad-content confirmation **[verify]**. UK: GB NHC claims only.
- **PDPA:** health information is highly sensitive. No custom audiences built from patient lists without explicit consent. No copy implying the viewer has a condition.

## 8. Ten rules for every TITAN plan
1. Fill in the diagnosis before tactics.
2. List CEPs per brand and brief 1–2 per campaign.
3. Grow penetration: reach category buyers broadly, and target strategically within geographic limits.
4. Creative is about half the effect: invest in concepts, test big swings, use right-brain craft.
5. Keep 1–3 distinctive assets for years and show the brand in the first 3 seconds.
6. Set the brand/activation split by size and category; 60/40 is context-dependent.
7. Use a mid-tier creator portfolio and amplify the winners.
8. Measure incrementally (holdouts, lift tests, geo tests, then MMM). Never trust last-click alone.
9. SG health: compliance comes before creative.
10. Every result goes to `/learn`.
