# Buyer psychology and copywriting

Applies to every script, caption, ad, landing page, email, WhatsApp message and carousel, for every brand. Researched 2026-10-25; sources are in `research/sources-agency-playbooks.md` §1.

**Precedence:** ledger > brand file > compliance pack > this file. **Compliance beats persuasion every time.**

**Evidence labels:**
- **[S]** strong
- **[M]** moderate
- **[C]** contested or failed replication
- **[RoT]** practitioner rule of thumb

## 1. Decision psychology: what works, how well, and how to use it
| # | Lever | Evidence | Apply | Misuse / SG limits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Processing fluency / System 1** (easy = true, liked) | [M-S] Alter & Oppenheimer 2009. "Disfluency makes people think harder" failed a 17-lab replication (Meyer et al. 2015) | One idea per frame or slide; high-contrast captions; short concrete words; rhythm and rhyme in lines ("Sit less. Move more. Hurt less."); the same brand codes every time | Making a weak claim feel true through repetition is still misleading |
| 2 | **Loss aversion and framing** | [C→M] Real but moderated (Mrkva et al. 2020): weaker in experts, stronger in novices and older buyers. Framing replicates (Many Labs) | Loss frame for "the cost of doing nothing" with first-time buyers; gain frame for prevention and habits; A/B test both | Health fear-mongering: SCAP and HCSA bar alarmist content |
| 3 | **Anchoring** | [S] when anchors are relevant (Many Labs). Arbitrary anchors give mixed results | Show the premium tier first; anchor to the cost of the alternative and to time ("10 min a day") | Fake "was" prices: CCS acted in May 2026. SG health: **exact final price only**, no original or comparative price |
| 4 | **Decoy** | [C] Works when every attribute is numeric; fails when attributes are perceptual (Frederick et al. 2014) | Only in numeric pricing tables (tub sizes, retainer tiers); always test | Fake tiers that nobody is meant to buy |
| 5 | **Choice overload** | [C] Near-zero average effect; real when moderators apply (Chernev et al. 2015: hard decision, complex set, unclear preferences) | One CTA per asset; ≤3 tiers; a "Recommended" badge; route by symptom or goal ("Back / Neck / Sports / Pelvic") | Hiding options people need |
| 6 | **Defaults** | [S] d≈0.68 (Jachimowicz et al. 2019), stronger when the default reads as an endorsement | Pre-select the recommended plan and the common appointment type | Pre-ticked add-ons; auto-renew traps; marketing consent must be opt-in (PDPA, GDPR); CCS "sneaking into basket" |
| 7 | **Endowment** | [M] | Samples, trials, "build your plan" configurators, personalised reports, the prospect's own data in a mock dashboard (AI agency) | Trials that convert silently into paid plans |
| 8 | **Goal-gradient / endowed progress** | [M-S] Kivetz 2006; Nunes & Drèze 2006 | Progress bars that start partly done; carousel counters "3/7"; streaks and 28-day plans | Manipulative streaks (EU Digital Fairness Act proposal due Q4 2026) |
| 9 | **Peak-end** | [M] | End videos on the payoff, not a logo slate; end each clinic visit with a clear home plan and a follow-up text | — |
| 10 | **Mere exposure / mental availability** | [S] Zajonc; Ehrenberg-Bass | Consistent distinctive assets; appear at category entry points (marketing-effectiveness.md) | Frequency without variation causes wear-out |
| 11 | **Curiosity gap** (Loewenstein information-gap) | [M]. The Zeigarnik "unfinished tasks are remembered" effect replicates poorly, so use open loops for attention, not memory | Open a *specific* gap and close it inside the asset ("Your glutes might be why your knee hurts. 20-second test →") | Clickbait that never pays off; misleading thumbnails |
| 12 | **Social proof** | [M] depends on context: works best with similar people, specific numbers, plus an injunctive norm (towel-study replications are mixed) | Non-health: specific counts, people like the reader, recency, UGC, reviews. **SG health:** population statistics with a source, guideline consensus, capacity facts, technical accreditations (JCI/ISO), community engagement | Fake counters ("23 viewing"; CCS 2026); fake or incentivised reviews (UK DMCC since Apr 2025); **no testimonials or reshared reviews for SG health** |
| 13 | **Authority** | [S], and stronger in high power-distance SEA | Accurate credentials ("Registered physiotherapist, AHPC"); clinician on camera; guideline citations; *demonstrate* expertise by teaching something non-obvious | "Specialist" unless formally recognised; paid awards (MOH: up to S$20k fine and/or jail); AI likenesses of real experts |
| 14 | **Liking** | [S] | Founder-led content; consistent faces; local accent and Singlish where the voice fits; behind the scenes; people's names | Undisclosed paid relationships (ASAS) |
| 15 | **Reciprocity** | [M-S] | Genuinely useful free education: exercise sheet, 3-move routine, AI-audit checklist | **SG health: an advertised free consult or free gift is an inducement.** Give information, not freebies |
| 16 | **Commitment / consistency** | [S] in individualist cultures; weaker in collectivist ones, which follow what peers did (Cialdini et al. 1999; Petrova et al. 2007) | Micro-yes ladders (quiz → result → book); public challenge pledges; in SEA pair with "what people like you did" | Using the sunk cost of a long quiz to push people at a paywall |
| 17 | **Unity** | [M] | Tribe language: "for SG office workers", "for mums getting back to lifting" | Exclusionary "us vs them" |
| 18 | **Scarcity / urgency** | [M] context-dependent (Barton et al. 2022 meta-analysis). Stacking quantity and time limits added nothing (Ladeira 2023) | Only real constraints (cohort start, batch size, event date), with the reason stated ("8 spots: 1 coach per 8 women") | Fake timers, random "almost sold out", sales that never end (CCS May 2026). **No urgency at all in SG health ads** |
| 19 | **Risk reversal** | [M] | Supplements: money-back guarantee. Fitness: no lock-in. AI agency: milestone pricing | **Never guarantee clinical outcomes.** Reverse the *process* risk instead: "pay per session, no packages pushed" |

## 2. Motivation models: use one per brief
- **Jobs to be Done (Moesta's forces).** A switch happens when push + pull outweigh anxiety + habit.
  - Interview recent buyers ("what was happening the day you decided?").
  - Write one line per force.
  - Every asset either amplifies push and pull, or reduces anxiety and habit. Most ads forget anxiety, so add price shown up front, an FAQ, or "no package pressure".
- **Schwartz awareness × sophistication.** Tag every brief with both, and match the opening to the level.
  - **Awareness levels:**
    - Unaware → story or identity
    - Problem-aware → name the problem in their words
    - Solution-aware → the mechanism
    - Product-aware → proof and offer
    - Most aware → the deal and the CTA
  - **Sophistication stages:** 1 simple claim → 2 bigger claim → 3 new mechanism → 4 improved mechanism → 5 identity and experience.
  - Where our categories sit:
    - SG protein and fitness = stage 4–5 (sell identity, community and taste, not "25 g protein")
    - Men's pelvic health = often unaware, so lead with education and normalising
    - AI agencies = stage 3–4, so name a distinctive mechanism ("14-day automation sprint")
- **Hormozi value equation:** (dream outcome × perceived likelihood) ÷ (time delay × effort).
  - Paint the outcome concretely.
  - Raise likelihood with mechanism, credentials and process transparency.
  - Shorten time to the first win. For clinics: "leave your first session with a plan", not a result promise.
  - Cut effort: WhatsApp booking, a 3-exercise routine, done-for-you.
- **Fogg B = MAP.** Raise *ability* before chasing motivation: one-tap WhatsApp with a prefilled message, price and slots visible, map pin. Prompt at the moment of need.
- **Identity-based buying.** Use noun labels ("for runners who refuse to stop"). The customer is the hero and the brand is the guide. Never shame people who don't fit (body image).
- **Desire mapping [RoT].** Map the offer to safety/health, belonging, esteem/status, competence or self-expression. Use it as a checklist, not Maslow's ladder.

## 3. Copy frameworks: pick one per asset
| Framework | Use for | Skeleton |
|---|---|---|
| **AIDA** | any cold ad | attention → why care → want → act |
| **PAS** | problem-aware audience | problem → agitate (factual for health) → solution |
| **BAB** | behavioural change (never clinical results) | before → after → bridge |
| **4Ps** | landing pages | promise → picture → proof → push |
| **PASTOR** | long sales pages | problem → amplify → story → transformation → offer → response. *Strip the testimony for health* |
| **QUEST** | email | qualify → understand → educate → stimulate → transition |
| **FAB** | product pages | feature → advantage → benefit ("25 g isolate → mixes clear → no bloated feeling mid-meeting") |
| **ABT** | hooks, captions, B2B | "X and Y, but Z, therefore W" (storytelling-playbook.md) |

**Craft rules:**
- **One big idea.** If you can't state it in ≤7 words, you have two ads.
- **Specificity beats adjectives.** "Mixes in 4 shakes", "physio-led 45-minute assessment". Precise numbers read as more credible [M].
- **Headlines.** Lead with self-interest (Caples); put the benefit and the news in the headline (Ogilvy); a long, specific headline can win.
  - Formulas:
    - "How to [outcome] without [pain]"
    - "[N] [things] [audience] should know"
    - "The [mechanism] that…"
    - "Warning: [common mistake]" (never health fear)
- **Social hooks (0–3 s).** The first frame works on its own: visual surprise + on-screen text + spoken line. Name the audience, open a gap, or take a contrarian stance. Captions on. Crude hooks go in post only (studio rule).
- **Voice of customer (VOC) mining.**
  - Sources:
    - competitor reviews (1–3★ = pain, 5★ = outcomes)
    - Reddit (r/singapore, r/askSingapore), HardwareZone, Lowyat
    - TikTok and YouTube comments
    - WhatsApp enquiry logs, sales calls, Google "People also ask"
  - Tag phrases by force and awareness level. Lift "sticky" phrases verbatim into headlines.
  - Validate with paid hook tests before scaling.
  - **Health:** VOC shapes the *language*; it is never published as a patient quote.
- **Objection handling.** List the top 5 (price, time, "will it work for me", trust, embarrassment). Answer them in-line, in an FAQ, or voiced in video ("You're probably thinking…"). Men's health: address embarrassment directly (private room, clinician gender choice).
- **CTA writing.** First person or benefit ("Get my plan", "See available times"). Name the next step and its size ("2-minute quiz"). One primary CTA. Put an anxiety reducer beside it ("No payment to book"). Health CTAs stay neutral: "Book an assessment", "WhatsApp us".
- **Readability.**
  - Grade 6–8 for consumers, 8–10 for B2B.
  - Sentences of ≤20 words; front-loaded headings; bullets.
  - People scan in F and layer-cake patterns and read ~20–28% of a page (NN/g).
  - Plain English matters more for SEA second-language readers.
- **Microcopy.** Say why each field is needed. Error messages should explain how to fix it. Show total costs early: unexpected costs are Baymard's #1 abandonment reason, and drip pricing is illegal in the UK under DMCC.

## 4. Offers
- **Formula.** Core product + speed-to-value + effort reducers + risk reversal + a reason why the price is what it is + a real limit.
  - Name it: audience + outcome + timeframe + container. Example: "PeachForm Strong-Start 28: your first 4 weeks of confident lifting".
- **Bonuses.** Each bonus removes a named objection. Stated values must be real stand-alone prices.
- **Pricing.**
  - Charm (.99) pricing works only when the left digit changes and is stronger side by side (Thomas & Morwitz 2005). Use it for value positioning; use round prices for premium.
  - Three tiers with the best value in the middle.
  - "$1.80 a shake" framing.
  - Bundles reduce price comparison.
  - Instalments and subscriptions reduce the pain of paying, but must allow easy exit (UK DMCC subscription rules).
- **SG clinics.** Packages may exist but **may not be advertised**; they are disclosed only at the point of payment. The advertised "offer" is the service and process: assessment + home plan, transparent final price.

## 5. Culture: SEA persuasion
- **Collectivism.** Family, in-group and harmony appeals; peer history beats personal consistency. Singapore is bicultural, so test individual vs family frames.
- **Trust in people you know.** Nielsen 2015: ~88% of SEA respondents trust recommendations from people they know, vs ~48–56% for social ads.
  - Build forwardable assets ("send this to your dad").
  - **SG clinics:** "share with someone who sits all day" with no reward. Referral rewards count as inducements.
- **Chat-first channels.** WhatsApp (SG/MY) and Zalo (VN): the primary CTA is a chat with a prefilled message and a fast human reply. PDPA and the DNC registry still apply to broadcasts.
- **Face / embarrassment.**
  - Use privacy cues and indirect language for stigmatised topics.
  - Never shame the viewer in hooks.
  - Comment CTAs fail for stigmatised topics; use DM or WhatsApp.
- **Kiasu.** "Don't get left behind" works for education, fitness and B2B AI. In SG health, turn it into *preparedness* ("know the 3 warning signs"), not scarcity.
- **Authority and hierarchy.** High power distance in SG/MY: credentialed experts, and the "uncle/auntie" archetypes, persuade. Avoid religious or celebrity endorsements of health products (MY backlash).
- **Language.** Singlish, Manglish and Vietnamese registers build liking, but match the brand's formality. MY MAB ads in other languages need certified translations.

## 6. Ethical persuasion for clinics (the substitute toolkit)
1. Education is the persuasion: teach the mechanism.
2. Authority through real credentials and cited guidelines.
3. Social proof without testimonials: population data, guideline consensus, accreditation, de-identified "a common pattern we see".
4. Risk reversal on process (transparent price, no lock-in), never on outcomes.
5. Lower friction: WhatsApp, visible slots, location, hours.
6. Label dramatisations; actors are never presented as patients.

## 7. Persuasion checklist (run on every asset; QC before /publish)
**Strategy**
1. Brand, market and compliance pack loaded; health and supplement claims flagged.
2. Awareness × sophistication tagged; the opening matches.
3. One big idea, ≤7 words.
4. JTBD: push and pull amplified, and at least one anxiety or habit reduced.
5. At least 2 VOC phrases used (as quotes only where legal).

**Hook and structure**

6. The first 1–3 s (or the headline) names the audience or problem, or opens a gap the asset closes.
7. One framework, used cleanly.
8. Specific numbers and concrete nouns.
9. Fluency: one idea per frame, grade 6–8 (B2B 8–10), captions on, high contrast.
10. Distinctive brand assets present.
11. The ending is the peak (payoff, then CTA), not a dead slate.

**Proof and trust**

12. Authority is real, with sources logged in SOURCES.md.
13. Social proof is legal for the market. SG health: no testimonials, reshared reviews or before/after.
14. Every claim is substantiable; no health outcome guarantees; no disease claims for supplements.
15. No superlatives or comparatives for SG/MY health.

**Offer and CTA**

16. Value equation: outcome clear, likelihood raised, time-to-first-win shown, effort reduced.
17. Risk reversal present (process-based for clinics).
18. Honest pricing. SG health: exact final price only; no "from", strike-through, advertised packages or free consults.
19. Scarcity or urgency only if true, with the reason stated; none in SG health ads.
20. One primary CTA, the next step named, an anxiety reducer beside it (WhatsApp/Zalo).
21. Fair defaults and choice set: ≤3 options, no pre-ticked add-ons, opt-in consent.

**Ethics, culture and disclosure**

22. Cultural fit: family frame considered, face-saving language, localised.
23. Paid relationships disclosed (#ad; VN influencer duties; MY KKLIU number where required).
24. Dark-pattern sweep: no hidden fees, confirm-shaming, cancellation friction, fake counters or manipulative streaks.
25. Dramatisations labelled; no AI likeness of real people; fear proportionate. Then QC and `/learn`.
