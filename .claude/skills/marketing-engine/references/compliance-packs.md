# Compliance packs (industry × market)
Pick one industry pack + one market pack per brand and copy the rules that bite into `brands/<brand>.md` §2/§9. These are working summaries, not legal advice. Check the named regulator's current code before a launch, and when in doubt cut the claim. Platform ad policies (Meta, TikTok, Google, YouTube) apply on top of the law and are often stricter.

## A. Universal rules (every brand, every market)
- **Claims:** only claims you can prove, with evidence held on file. No "best/#1/guaranteed" unless independently verified and dated.
- **Reviews and testimonials:** never fake them, never pay for undisclosed ones, never edit them to change meaning.
- **Endorsements:** paid creators disclose clearly ("#ad", "paid partnership" tools).
- **Prices and offers:** state real terms, end dates and conditions. No fake scarcity.
- **People:** no AI likeness or voice of real people without consent. No children in ads for adult products.
- **Privacy:** get consent before collecting leads, honour opt-outs, and keep data only as long as needed.
- **IP:** use licensed music, fonts and stock only. Never copy other creators' content; study the technique instead.

## B. Industry packs
### Health, medical, allied health, clinics
- No testimonials or before/after implying guaranteed results where the market bans them (SG, MY and most of the EU are strict).
- No superlatives or comparisons with other practitioners.
- Inducements are restricted: free consults, discounts or bundles are banned in some markets.
- No claims to cure, and no fear-mongering.
- Practitioners appear only with their registered title.
- Dramatised skits must not pose as real patients. Sexual-health topics need clinical, non-explicit wording.

### Supplements and nutrition
- Only approved, permitted health claims. No disease claims ("treats, cures, prevents").
- No weight-loss promises with numbers or timeframes unless the market allows and evidence supports them.
- Testimonials can't imply typical results.

### Fitness, weight loss and body image
- No unrealistic transformations, "lose X kg in Y days" claims, or shaming.
- Before/after content is often restricted on Meta and TikTok ads.
- Age-gate weight-loss ads (18+).

### Beauty and cosmetics
- Cosmetic claims must stay cosmetic ("looks smoother", not "repairs DNA").
- Disclose filters and retouching where required, as in UK guidance on filtered images in ads.
- Aesthetic medical procedures fall under the health pack.

### Finance, investing, crypto, insurance
- Show risk warnings. No guaranteed returns, and past performance comes with a disclaimer.
- Only licensed entities can promote regulated products, and finfluencers must disclose.
- Crypto ads are heavily restricted or banned on many platforms and in many markets.

### Alcohol, tobacco, vape and gambling
- Age-gate everything and keep audiences adult-only. Never link the product to success, sex or driving.
- Many platforms and markets ban or limit these ads outright. Gambling needs a licence and responsible-gaming messaging.

### Food and beverage
- Truthful photos, honest portion and price info, and allergen accuracy.
- Children's marketing is restricted for high fat, sugar and salt foods (UK, EU and others).
- Halal or kosher claims only when certified.

### Real estate and property
- Accurate prices, sizes and renders, with "artist's impression" labels.
- Licensed agents only, with licence numbers where required.
- No discriminatory targeting under Meta's special ad category for housing.

### Education, courses and coaching
- No income or result guarantees. Show typical results, not outliers.
- Accreditation claims must be true, and refund terms must be clear.

### Legal and professional services
- Professional body rules apply: no guarantees, comparisons or testimonials where barred.
- Advertising by lawyers is restricted in many jurisdictions.

### Dating, relationships and sexual wellness
- Adult audience only. Non-explicit creative, and no sexualised minors or ambiguity about age.
- Platforms restrict sexual-wellness ads heavily: use clinical, educational framing and never algospeak (respellings or slang used to dodge moderation).

### Employment and recruitment
- No discriminatory requirements. Meta's special ad category applies to employment ads.

### Media, publishers and community pages (news, tips, scam exposés) (added 2026-10-25, verify)
- Label every sponsored, partner or affiliate post as an ad, with the platform tool plus a visible word in the post. A page that sells posts carries the same duties as an influencer.
- Scam and crime exposés describe patterns. Name or show a person or business only when an official source has (police, court, or reputable press citing police), and link it. Otherwise it is a defamation risk.
- Blur bystanders' faces, number plates, phone numbers and identifying shop signs. Label recreations "dramatisation".
- Cite a source line for every news fact. Don't call an unlicensed page "press", "official" or a "newspaper".
- No rage-bait framing against a nationality, ethnicity or trade. Punch at the trick, not the group.
- Sensational crime or scam creative is often rejected as a paid ad. Run it organically and keep paid creative calm.

### Travel, tourism and experiences (added 2026-10-25, verify)
- Quote prices, inclusions, cancellation and refund terms exactly as at checkout. No fake scarcity.
- Don't call a service a "tour" or "travel agency" unless it holds the market's tourism licence.
- Location and drone rules: check venue commercial-shoot fees and drone permits before promising a location or shot.
- Use customer photos in ads only with explicit ad consent; a portfolio opt-out is not enough.

### Vietnam detail (added 2026-10-25 for snap-saigon; verify against official texts before launch)
- **Ad and sponsored labelling, KOL duties:**
  - The amended Advertising Law No. 75/2025/QH15 has applied since 1 Jan 2026.
  - Social media users must mark ads and sponsored content.
  - Influencers (KOLs/KOCs) must check the advertiser, only promote what they have used or clearly understand, and disclose before and during the promotion.
  - Violating ads must come down within 24 h of a request from the authorities.
  - Sources: Tilleke & Gibbins 2025-07-09 https://www.tilleke.com/insights/vietnam-amends-key-aspects-of-advertising-law/ · VietNamNet https://vietnamnet.vn/en/online-influencers-face-tighter-rules-in-vietnam-s-ad-overhaul-2427991.html
- **Language:**
  - Advertising Law 2012 Art. 18: ad content must be in Vietnamese; brand names, slogans and trade names are exempt.
  - A foreign language sits below the Vietnamese at no more than 3/4 of its size, and in video the Vietnamese comes first.
  - Source: https://avia.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Vietnam-2024-Advertising-Matrix-_Final.pdf
- **Image rights:**
  - Civil Code 2015 Art. 32 requires consent to use a person's image, with payment if the use is commercial.
  - The Advertising Law bars using someone's image in an ad without consent.
  - Source: https://lawnet.vn/thong-tin-phap-luat/en/tu-van-luat/is-the-use-of-other-people-s-images-without-permission-subject-to-compensation-in-vietnam-102840.html
- **False or slanderous posts:**
  - Decree 15/2020 Art. 101 (as amended) fines posting false, distorted or slanderous information. Criminal slander and defamation liability apply on top.
  - Source: https://www.tilleke.com/insights/new-penalties-posting-fake-news-social-networks/
- **Accounts:** Decree 147/2024 (from 25 Dec 2024) says only phone-verified social accounts may post. Source: https://vietnamnews.vn/society/1666838/social-network-users-must-authenticate-accounts-from-december-25.html
- **Personal data:**
  - The Personal Data Protection Law 91/2025/QH15 has applied since 1 Jan 2026, with implementing Decree 356/2025.
  - Consent must be explicit and specific, which covers lead forms and WhatsApp capture.
  - Source: https://kpmg.com/vn/en/home/insights/2025/06/vietnam-new-personal-data-protection-law.html
- **Sensitive topics:** avoid political, state and leader content. Health, food and cosmetics ads may need permits (MOH).

## D. Before publishing (the 30-second check)
1. Is every claim provable and in the brand's permitted list?
2. Does it break any "don't" in the brand file §9?
3. Are there testimonials, results, prices or offers? If so, check the market rules.
4. Are there people? Check consent, age and likeness.
5. Is the platform category restricted (health, finance, dating, alcohol, weight loss)? Use compliant wording, never a workaround.
