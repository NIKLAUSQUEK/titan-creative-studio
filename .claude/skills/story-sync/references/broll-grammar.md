# B-roll grammar

**A-roll** carries the story (talking head, interview, dialogue, presenter). **B-roll** is anything cut over or between it while the A-roll audio usually continues.

## Types and their emotional job [RoT synthesis]
| Type | Job | Use / avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Literal / illustrative | clarity, proof (show exactly what's said) | explainers, UGC; avoid redundancy (a phone for every "phone") |
| Metaphor / abstract | feeling (clock = pressure, storm = anger) | docs, manifestos, luxury edits |
| Insert / detail | intimacy, credibility (hands, texture, screen) | clinic process, product, tutorials |
| Establishing | place/time, resets the viewer between chapters | docs, vlogs, location changes |
| Reaction | tells the audience how to feel | comedy and drama's strongest cue |
| Archival | authenticity, nostalgia | founder stories, docs (source it) |
| Screen recording | proof | software, AI agency, tutorials |
| Stock | cheap, fast | generic risk: handshakes, pointing at screens, lightbulbs = never |
| Meme insert | self-aware wink | personal/creator accounts only; never health/premium |
| Macro product | desire, ASMR | protein, apparel, product hero |
| Data / graphic | authority | must be sourced; health claims substantiated |

## When to cut to B-roll [P/RoT]
1. **On the key noun/verb as it is spoken** (or 2-4 frames before) so image and word land together.
2. **To hide jump cuts** left by filler removal (doc editors cover with B-roll or gesture shots, PremiumBeat).
3. **To change energy** at a section boundary.
4. **To show, not tell**: if the line describes something visual, show it.
5. **To cover weak delivery** while keeping the strong audio.
6. **Never over the speaker's most emotional or most credible moment** - hold the face (Murch: emotion 51%).
Cut on action (a glance, a shift in the chair) for invisible cuts.

## J-cuts and L-cuts
- **J-cut**: next shot's audio starts before its picture → curiosity pulls forward. Default into new scenes/chapters.
- **L-cut**: current audio continues over the next picture → feeling lingers. Default out of emotional lines.
- Pattern: speaker audio over B-roll, then raise the B-roll's natural sound gradually (Vimeo guide).

## Motivated vs unmotivated cuts
Motivated = triggered by an eyeline, sound, movement or question. Unmotivated (jarring) only on purpose for shock/comedy/pattern interrupt, and then make it visibly deliberate (sound hit or big framing change) or it reads as an error.

## Duration by format [RoT - no empirical standard; vary lengths]
| Format | B-roll shot length |
|---|---|
| Talking-head short | 0.7-2 s |
| Explainer | 1.5-3 s (any graphic readable twice) |
| Documentary | 3-6 s; emotional/landscape 6-10 s |
| Premium / health ad | 2-4 s; macro product 1.5-3 s |
| Trailer montage | 0.5-1.5 s |
Average shot length in films fell from ~8-10 s (1960s) to ~3-4 s (2005); post-1980 films vary shot length in a 1/f "attention-like" pattern (Cutting, DeLong & Nothelfer 2010, *Psych. Science*). **Vary, don't metronome.**

## A-roll : B-roll share [RoT]
Talking-head short 60-80 : 20-40 (the face builds trust) · UGC ad 70-90 : 10-30 (polish breaks authenticity) · explainer 30-50 : 50-70 · mini-doc 40-50 : 50-60 · brand ad 10-40 : 60-90.

## B-roll that hurts
Too literal (picture mickey-mousing) · stock clichés · mismatched generic footage · so much B-roll the speaker disappears (montage) · B-roll with fast motion, text or faces competing with the VO for eye-trace.

## Studio specifics
- Real clinic/staff/product B-roll beats generated; generated B-roll only for the impossible/expensive (ai-video: hybrid workflows).
