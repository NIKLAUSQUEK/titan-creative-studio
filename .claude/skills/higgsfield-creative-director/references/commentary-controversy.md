# Cultural commentary, controversy & social-justice storytelling

## Topics in scope
Real-world discourse people are drawn to: creator-economy and OnlyFans economics, manosphere/sexism discourse (e.g. Andrew Tate-style rhetoric), religion-and-influencer controversies (e.g. Sneako), tech-billionaire edginess (e.g. Elon Musk), dating culture, masculinity, body image, wealth gaps, racism and discrimination people actually experience, immigrant life, class, health inequity.

## Formats
- **Receipts breakdown**: claim → the actual clip/post (screenshot or short excerpt) → context → what the data/source says → verdict. Commentator on camera (Klaus or persona archetype).
- **Video essay / explainer**: chaptered, B-roll + text callouts + sourced stats.
- **Reaction / duet / stitch**: short excerpt of a public clip + transformative commentary.
- **Debate split-screen**: steelman both sides fairly, then your take.
- **Street interview / vox pop**: real people, consented, released.
- **Satire sketch**: FICTIONAL archetype characters ("a manosphere guru", "a billionaire poster") — never an AI likeness or voice of the real person.
- **Lived-experience story**: told by people from the community (real interviews or clearly fictional dramatisation built from documented experiences).

## Fact-check layer (mandatory for real people/events)
1. Web-search every factual claim; store sources in `projects/<p>/SOURCES.md` (link, date, quote ≤15 words).
2. Never fabricate quotes, events, numbers, or motives. Attribute ("according to…", "in a 2025 stream he said…").
3. Label opinion as opinion ("my take", "I think"); facts must be verifiable.
4. Steelman the other side once before the critique — credibility and less "biased" pushback.
5. Real people: no AI likeness, AI voice clone, or synthetic "footage" of them (deepfake + defamation + platform policy). Use real public clips (short, transformative, credited), screenshots of public posts, or text cards.
6. Legal: US — truth and clearly-stated opinion are defences, but false statements of fact about real people are not; UK and Singapore are stricter on defamation, and Singapore also has POFMA for falsehoods — for SG/UK publication keep claims tightly sourced. Short excerpts for commentary ≠ guaranteed fair use: keep clips brief, transformative, never the whole thing.
7. Critiquing a hateful ideology is fine; repeating its slurs/talking points without challenge is not — always pair with rebuttal or context.

## Social-justice storytelling rules
- Centre the people affected: their words, their consent, their framing. Cast and consult from the community.
- Use credible data (government stats, peer-reviewed studies, reputable NGOs) and cite it.
- Show systems and specifics, not stereotypes; avoid "trauma porn" (gratuitous suffering for views) — show dignity and agency.
- Offer a path: resources, what viewers can do, who's doing the work.
- Avoid misinformation even when it would help the cause — it gets the whole message discredited.

## Gate hooks
Gate 1: "Does this involve real people or real events?" → yes triggers the fact-check layer + SOURCES.md before Gate 3. Gate 3 compliance block lists sources, opinion labels, clip usage, and likeness rules.

## Sensitive true stories across racial/religious backgrounds (e.g. UK grooming-gang scandals)
Goal: tell the truth fully — perpetrators, victims, institutional failures — without making a whole ethnicity, religion or culture the culprit.

**Framing rules**
- Responsibility sits with the specific perpetrators (named only where convicted and lawful to name) and the institutions that failed (police, councils, social services) — never "the X community" or "X culture".
- Ethnicity/religion is stated only where documented and relevant, using the official sources' own wording (e.g. inquiry/audit findings, court records), with context: the reports themselves, the scale and the limits of the data.
- Include voices from within the same communities who condemned the crimes, reported them, or campaign against them — shows the diversity within any group.
- Name the documented institutional failures directly, including where official reports found that fear of being called racist contributed to inaction — accuracy cuts both ways.
- No numbers without a source; no extrapolating from one case to a population.
- Don't feed hate: no imagery or language that mirrors extremist content (mobs, slurs, "invasion" framing); no calls to action against groups; comments plan includes moderation.

**Culture-blame test (run on every script line)**
1. Swap the group noun for the specific actor (the convicted men / the council / the officers) — if the line still works, use the specific version.
2. Would a viewer finish believing the whole group is responsible? → rewrite.
3. Is every claim about the group sourced from an official/peer-reviewed source and quoted in context?
4. Is there at least one voice from that community inside the story?

**Survivor-centred, child-protection rules (non-negotiable)**
- Victims were children: NO depiction, re-enactment or AI recreation of abuse; nothing sexual or suggestive; minors never shown in any sexualised context. Use symbolic visuals (empty bus stop at night, phone screen glow, closed doors, case files, empty playground, silhouettes of adults/officials), documents, data graphics, adult survivors speaking by their own choice.
- UK law: victims of sexual offences have lifelong anonymity — never name or show anything that could identify them (location + age + detail can identify). Check contempt-of-court risk for live proceedings before publishing.
- No AI likeness of real victims or perpetrators. Real convicted offenders: public-record photos/court artists only where lawful.
- Trauma-informed: consent from any survivor featured, their framing, no graphic detail, content warning up front, helpline/resources at the end (UK: NSPCC, Childline, NAPAC; local equivalents per market — verify current numbers).

**Sources to anchor on** (verify current versions at research time): official inquiry and audit reports, court judgments/sentencing remarks, parliamentary records, established investigative journalism. Store in SOURCES.md.
