# Villains, cruelty & conflict — depict honestly, never glorify

Principle: show it real, show it hurt, show what it costs. Controversy and cruelty are fair game in story; what we never do is let the edit reward the cruelty.

## Villain design (no cartoon slop)
- **Want + wound + method**: what they want, what fear drives it, how they get it (money, status, charm, humiliation).
- **Believable specifics**: the exact line, the smirk, the audience they perform it for, the group-chat screenshot.
- **One human crack** (a moment we understand them) — makes the fall land harder.
- Archetypes: the one-upping rich bully, the status-climber who looks down, the manipulative partner, the mean-girl ringleader, the slut-shaming gossip, the abusive boss, the sneering landlord, the online pile-on leader, the "just joking" bully, the hypocrite moraliser.

## Hero / target design
Real stakes, real reaction (not instant comebacks), agency (a choice, not just endurance), allies who show up (or don't — that's a story too).

## Cruelty scenes we can write (adult characters for anything sexual)
Wealth mockery and one-upping · humiliation in public · slut-shaming and double standards · body-shaming · bullying (in-person and online pile-ons) · degrading treatment at work · gaslighting and manipulation · class contempt · racist incidents depicted as the wrong they are (from the target's experience; slurs avoided or used once, sparingly, only if the story truly needs it and never as a laugh line).
Sexual-themed shaming/degradation = adult characters only (25+). School bullying stories may include teen characters, never anything sexual.

## Craft rules: depiction ≠ glorification
1. **Camera allegiance**: hold on the target's face; the audience feels the hit, not the thrill.
2. **No victory music for cruelty**: villain moments get cold sound — room tone drop, dissonance, silence; no hype stims, no slow-mo swagger on the bully.
3. **Show impact**: the after-moment (bathroom, car, 3am phone glow) — one beat is enough.
4. **Consequence lands** — choose the ending honestly:
   - *Downfall*: exposure, the tables turn, losing what they valued (status, partner, job, friends).
   - *Karma twist*: the thing they mocked happens to them.
   - *Realistic*: they face nothing, but we see the cost they don't see — and the target's growth wins.
   - *Redemption*: earned, specific, not instant.
   - *Open*: "who was right?" — only if the cruelty itself isn't presented as right.
5. **Audience side-choosing (comment bait)**: end on a choice ("Should she have forgiven him?", "Team Mira or Team Jade?"), pinned poll, Part 2 tease.
6. **The joke never lands on the victim's pain** — comedy can roast the villain, the absurdity of the situation, or the hypocrisy.

## Story shapes
Revenge glow-up · the exposé (receipts drop) · the bystander who finally speaks · the villain's POV that indicts itself · the twist where the "villain" was the victim · the class one-up that backfires · the group-chat screenshot story.

## Gate hooks
Gate 2 directions may carry a villain; each card states the villain's want/wound/method and the consequence type. Gate 3 marks cruelty beats with camera allegiance + sound treatment + aftermath beat. QC adds: "Does the edit reward the cruelty?" → if yes, FIX.
