# Storytelling playbook

This playbook goes deeper than `story-engine.md`, which keeps goals, trope stances, hook formulas and emotive language; use both. It applies to the story-writer, story-director, script-doctor, director and editor briefs. Researched 2026-10-25; sources in `research/sources-agency-playbooks.md` §5.

Evidence labels: **[S]** strong · **[M]** moderate · **[W/C]** weak or contested · **[H]** craft heuristic.

## 1. Why stories work, and what not to claim
| Mechanism | Evidence | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Narrative transportation (absorption into the story raises story-consistent beliefs and liking) | [S] Green & Brock 2000 | Build for transportation: one identifiable character, concrete sensory detail, a plausible world, no sales interruption mid-story |
| Persuasion effect size | [S] Braddock & Dillard 2016 meta-analysis: r ≈ .17–.23. **Real but small** | Story multiplies a good offer; it can't rescue a weak one |
| What drives transportation | [S] van Laer et al. 2014 (132 effects): identifiable characters, imaginable plot, verisimilitude | Specific people, places and objects |
| Memory | [M] Bower & Clark 1969 (narrative chaining improves recall) | **The "22× more memorable" claim has no source. Never use it** |
| Oxytocin / empathy | [W/C] The trust result replicated poorly (Nave, Camerer & McCullough 2015) | **Never cite "the oxytocin effect"** |
| Ad effectiveness | [M-S] IPA (Binet & Field): emotional campaigns are about 2× as likely to produce top-box long-term profit. System1 star ratings predict long-term growth; right-brain features (characters, place, dialogue, humour, music) do better | Brand work leans emotional; activation can be rational (marketing-effectiveness.md) |
| Virality | [S] Berger & Milkman 2012: high-arousal emotions (awe, anger, anxiety, amusement) drive sharing more than sadness or contentment | Target a high-arousal peak for share-driven pieces |
| Neural coupling | [M] Stephens et al. 2010 | — |
| Sinek's "Why" | Communication order is useful; the limbic-brain biology is unsupported | Use why → how → what; don't cite the neuroscience |

## 2. Structures, and when to use each
| Model | Beats | Use for | Avoid when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **3-act / Aristotle** (reversal + recognition) | setup → confrontation → resolution | default for 30 s and longer | — |
| Freytag | exposition → rising → climax → falling → dénouement | cautionary tales | short form (falling action kills retention) |
| **Hero's Journey** (Campbell/Vogler, 12 stages) | ordinary world → call → refusal → mentor → threshold → tests → ordeal → reward → road back → resurrection → return | founder docs, brand films, long transformations | under 60 s (becomes cliché) |
| **Story Circle** (Harmon) | you → need → go → search → find → take (pay a price) → return → change | episodic series, skits (~⅛ of runtime per step) | — |
| **Save the Cat** (Snyder, 15 beats) | catalyst ~10%, break into 2 ~20–25%, midpoint 50%, all is lost ~75%, finale | short films, YouTube docs over 8 min | ads under 60 s |
| **Story Spine** (Adams; Coats' Pixar rule #4) | Once upon a time… Every day… One day… Because of that ×2… Until finally… Ever since… | ideation, skits, explainers, carousels | — |
| **Kishōtenketsu** | ki (intro) → shō (develop) → **ten (twist/juxtaposition)** → ketsu (reconcile); no villain needed | SEA festive and heartwarming spots, slice of life, 4-panel carousels | hard sell |
| **StoryBrand SB7** | customer (hero) has a problem → meets a guide (empathy + authority) → plan → call to action → avoids failure → success | websites, sales pages, explainers, B2B | founder-as-hero content |
| **Duarte sparkline** | alternates what is / what could be → new bliss | keynotes, manifestos, LinkedIn essays | — |
| **Vonnegut / Reagan arcs** (6 shapes: rags-to-riches, riches-to-rags, man-in-a-hole, Icarus, Cinderella, Oedipus) | pick the emotional line first | man-in-a-hole = default ad shape; Cinderella = transformation | riches-to-rags alone |
| **Storyworthy 5-second moment** (Dicks) | the story is about one 5-second change; start at the opposite of the ending | founder stories, storytime, wisdom posts | — |
| **ABT** (Olson) | "X **and** Y, **but** Z, **therefore** W" | loglines, hooks, captions, data, B2B | — |

**Choosing by length:**
- Under 20 s: ABT or a single reversal.
- 20–60 s: Story Spine, or man-in-a-hole built around a 5-second moment.
- Festive: Kishōtenketsu.
- Sales or landing page: SB7.
- Manifesto or keynote: sparkline.
- Doc or series: Story Circle per episode, with Save the Cat or Vogler across the season.

**But/Therefore QC (Parker & Stone).** Read the beats joined by "and then". If that works, it's a list, not a story. Rewrite each join as "but" or "therefore".

## 3. Character
- **Want vs need (Truby).** The want is external and visible ("fit the wedding dress"). The need is internal ("stop tying worth to the scale"). The product serves the want; the story pays off the need.
- **Flaw / sacred flaw (Storr; Cron).** The character holds a flawed theory of how the world works, and the plot attacks it. Example: "rest is for the weak" → "rest is training".
- **Stakes.** Make them concrete. Not "your health" but "carrying your daughter at her wedding".
- **Specificity.** "Auntie Mei, 58, mahjong every Thursday, can't sit 4 hours now", not "elderly people with back pain".
- **Relatability over likeability.** The audience needs to understand the want and see effort. An early kind act ("save the cat") buys tolerance for flaws.
- **Antagonists.** Give them want, wound and method (villains-conflict.md). **Abstract villains** are safer and often stronger for brands:
  - pain, time, the 3 pm crash
  - a myth ("no pain no gain")
  - the old way (agencies billing hours)
  - gym-bro advice, shame
  - Personify the villain visually, e.g. a recurring red-glow motif for pain.
- **Customer as hero, brand as guide (Miller; Duarte).** The guide shows empathy plus authority (credentials, process), not boasting. Exception: founder content, where Klaus is the hero and his vulnerability earns him the right to guide later.

## 4. Scene craft
- **Every scene turns a value** (McKee). It moves + → − or − → +; if it doesn't, cut it. McKee's **gap**: the character expects one result and the world gives another. That gap is where story lives.
- **Conflict per scene:** inner (doubt), personal (spouse, coach) or extra-personal (system, time, pain).
- **2 + 2, not 4 (Stanton).** Let the audience infer. "She falls asleep at a red light, keys in hand" beats "she was exhausted". Make me care; mix anticipation with uncertainty; have a strong theme.
- **Subtext.** "You're going to the gym *again*?" means "I feel left behind."
- **Setups and payoffs (Chekhov).** Everything shown pays off, and every payoff is set up. Encode both in spec.json `hard_rules`: whose prop it is, and what stays hidden until the reveal (ledger).
- **Reversal + recognition together** (Aristotle) make the strongest moment.
- **Dramatic irony.** The audience knows what the character doesn't. Ideal for POV skits.
- **Comedy:**
  - setup → escalation → punchline
  - rule of three: pattern ×2, break on the 3rd
  - callbacks, which also create loops
  - the punchline word goes **last**, and the reveal lands on a cut
- **Tension tools:** ticking clock, open question, withheld information, "but" beats.

## 5. Short-form mechanics (15–90 s)
1. **Stakes in 3 s.** The hook holds a character, a want or problem, and tension, with visual, on-screen text and spoken line in the same frame.
2. **In medias res.** Open at the most intense frame, then rewind ("3 weeks ago…").
3. **Open loops.** Promise a payoff and delay it. Close one loop and open the next (a rehook) every 5–8 s. Kallaway: hook → a "dance" of context and conflict → closing hook.
4. **Compressed arcs.** Show the world in one image, skip the falling action, end within 1–2 s of the payoff.
5. **Payoff timing.** The main payoff lands at 80–90% of runtime. The CTA comes **after** the emotional peak, never before.
6. **Loops.** The last line feeds the first.
7. **Series.** "Part 1 of 5" plus an unresolved question; recurring characters build fluency.
8. **Honest cliffhangers.** "Was she wrong?" No fake polls in ads.
9. **Wisdom posts.** Mini-story (≤60% of runtime) → one-line lesson → application.
10. **Storytime.** Face to camera, B-roll as proof, stakes in the first sentence.
11. **POV.** Second person plus dramatic irony.
12. **Micro-doc.** A real consented person and place → verité detail → a reveal.

**Retention map** (story-sync `structure-rhythm.md`):
- 0–3 s: hook.
- 3–8 s: context plus the first "but".
- 30–40%: escalation or first payoff.
- 60–75%: lowest point or reversal.
- 85%: payoff.
- Last 10%: loop or CTA.

## 6. Brand and founder stories
- **Kindra Hall's four stories** are value, founder, purpose and customer. Each runs normal → explosion → new normal and needs an identifiable character, real emotion, a significant moment and specific details.
- **Origin story:** a specific moment (not "always passionate"), its cost, the belief it formed, and what the company now does differently because of it.
- **Vulnerability:** scars, not open wounds. Every failure comes with its lesson and the behaviour it changed.
- **Manifesto:** sparkline ("They say… We believe…"), one abstract enemy, one promise.
- **QC checklists:**
  - Heath SUCCESs: Simple, Unexpected, Concrete, Credible, Emotional, Stories.
  - Berger STEPPS: Social currency, Triggers, Emotion (high arousal), Public, Practical value, Stories.
- **SG health customer stories (Physio Spot, heal360).** MOH: no testimonials or endorsements, no paid reviews, and no laudatory statements "regardless of whether they are factually accurate". Safe patterns:
  - **Dramatised composites** labelled "Dramatisation. Not a real patient." No outcomes, no before/after, no "best".
  - **Process stories:** what an assessment actually involves.
  - **Myth as villain.**
  - **Clinician POV:** "what I wish patients knew".
  - The change is in **understanding or behaviour**, never a claimed clinical result.
- **B2B case study (TITAN.AI):** SB7 + ABT. The client's situation → the cost of the problem in numbers → the decision → implementation, *including a setback* → the measured result with method → the new normal. Client quotes only with written consent (ASAS truthfulness).
- **Data stories:** one number per beat, compared to something human ("4 h/week = a Netflix season a month"). Put the "but" in the data; source every figure.

## 7. Long-form and documentary
- **Story bible:** ABT logline, controlling idea (McKee: value + cause), character cards (want/need/wound/flaw/arc), world rules, tone references, visual locks, episode map.
- **Interview → structure:**
  1. Transcribe.
  2. Tag 5-second moments, turns, quotes and emotions.
  3. Find the spine (the one change).
  4. Paper-edit into acts.
  5. Write VO only to bridge gaps.
- **Interview for scenes, not opinions:** "Take me to the moment…", "What did the room smell like?", "What did you expect? What actually happened?"
- **Act breaks** (Save the Cat proportions): catalyst ~10% · commitment ~25% · midpoint 50% · all is lost ~75% · climax ~90%. On YouTube, add a re-engagement peak every ~3 min.
- **B-story:** a relationship that carries the theme and converges with the A-story at the climax.
- **Series:** each episode is a complete Story Circle, and its "change" step seeds the next episode's "need". Across the season: ep 1 call → tests → penultimate ordeal → finale return.

## 8. SEA cultural storytelling
- **Petronas festive films (Yasmin Ahmad, Leo Burnett).** Small, specific interracial or intergenerational kindness, gentle humour, no villain. Close to Kishōtenketsu. *Tan Hong Ming* won at Cannes 2008.
- **SG Chinese New Year.** Reunion and its tensions: loneliness (Pokka 2017), ideal vs reality (Caltex 2018), awkward relatives' questions (M&M's 2025).
- **Other festivals:**
  - **Hari Raya:** balik kampung, forgiveness (maaf zahir batin), mother's cooking.
  - **Deepavali:** light over darkness, rangoli, mother and daughter.
  - **National Day:** ordinary heroes (void deck, hawkers, NS).
- **Thai melodrama (Thai Life "Unsung Hero").** A repeating pattern of small acts, then a reveal of the invisible return; the brand appears only at the end.
- **Vietnam: Biti's Hunter "Đi để trở về"** (since Tết 2017). A festive platform run as a multi-year series, a new character each season in the same structure.
- **Rules:**
  - The reward is family or community change, not personal glory.
  - Themes: filial piety, sacrifice, face.
  - **Humour by market:**
    - SG: self-deprecation, kiasu irony, Singlish code-switching, auntie/uncle archetypes, punching up.
    - MY: warm multiracial banter.
    - VN: wordplay, Gen-Z meme speak.
  - **Festive tone:** a warm cry plus a laugh. No death imagery or the number 4 at CNY. Use cultural reviewers for Raya and Deepavali.
  - Subtitles in the target language. Load the localisation.md pack.

**Scaling to other lengths:** 15 s ≈ halve every segment. 60 s ≈ double. For 180 s, multiply by 6 and add the midpoint rehook. Full 15/60/180 tables are in the research notes.

### Story diagnosis (run before `/plan` and at QC)
1. **Want:** stated in one line by second 3 (short form) or the end of act 1?
2. **Obstacle:** a specific force against it? Every join is "but" or "therefore"?
3. **Stakes:** what concrete thing is lost?
4. **Specific moment:** a 5-second moment with sensory detail (place, object, words)?
5. **Change:** a value shift (− → +) in the character or the viewer's belief?
6. **Emotional peak:** at what timestamp, and before the CTA?
7. **Setups and payoffs:** all paid off, all set up, encoded in `hard_rules`?
8. **2 + 2:** does the audience infer anything?
9. **Takeaway:** repeatable in one sentence? Is the brand the guide (except founder content)?
10. **Retention:** hook 0–3 s, rehook every 5–8 s, payoff at 80–90%, loop or clean close?
11. **Culture and compliance:** locale pack applied? SG health: no testimonial, laudatory claim or outcome; dramatisation labelled?
12. **Evidence:** every stat sourced? None of the oxytocin, "22×" or limbic claims?
